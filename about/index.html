Hands and eyes for the trees

A life in the trees needs grasping hands. Primates have nails instead of claws, and tactile pads with fingerprint ridges called dermatoglyphs, which help them grip.

Correct. Dermatoglyphs are the ridges on the tactile pads. Ischial callosities are the sitting pads of Old World monkeys.

How it works

From scattered to studied,
in four steps.

01

Gather

A professor shares what the class already has: lecture slides, readings, worksheets, in-class questions, links and films. Nothing needs to be reformatted first.

02

Organize

Every file is sorted by week and topic and kept in an archive, so the original material is always one tap away.

03

Write

The material becomes short reading passages and practice questions. Each question carries an explanation and a label saying where it came from.

04

Study

Students read, practice by subject, sit mock exams and return to what they missed. Their progress is saved as they go.

Who it's for

One class. Two points of view.

For students

Know what to study, and whether you know it.

  • Read in minutes, not hours. The class as short passages, with a question or two after each, and reading settings that are easy on the eyes.
  • Practice with answers that teach. Drills by subject, with an explanation for every question and a note on why the option you picked was wrong.
  • Rehearse the exam. A fresh mock exam whenever you want one, drawn from the whole class.
  • Come back to what you missed. Flag anything for review. Missed and flagged questions come up first next time.
For professors

Your material, working harder for your students.

  • Share what you already have. Slides, readings and worksheets as they are. They are organized by week into an archive students can open.
  • Questions that stay faithful to your class. Practice is written from your material, and every question says which slide, reading or activity it came from.
  • A class code, not another account. Students enter one code to open the class. There is nothing to install and nobody has to sign up.
  • A place for discussion. Post a question for the week and read every student's response in one thread. This part is in beta.

What's inside a class

Everything a student reaches for
the week before an exam.

Read & LearnThe class as short chapters of passages, each followed by questions.
DrillsMultiple-choice practice by subject or by source, with progress for every question.
Mock examA new practice exam each time, mixed from the whole class.
Review listEverything flagged or missed, gathered in one place.
Word rootsThe Greek and Latin behind the vocabulary, so terms stop being arbitrary.
ArchiveThe original slides, readings and worksheets, week by week.

What each class includes depends on the material it was built from. Practice questions are written as a study aid from the class's own material; they are not the professor's exam questions.
